# Licence — Contribution Run

The ten tracks of *Contribution Run* by Neon Husky are released under
**Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)**.

<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>

## In plain terms

You may use this music in videos, streams, games, podcasts, adverts and
anything else — **commercially or not** — provided you give credit.

Copy this:

```
Music: Neon Husky — "Contribution Run"
https://neonhusky.dev — licensed CC BY 4.0
```

You may also cut, loop, remix and build on it. You do not need to ask, pay,
or wait for a reply.

## If you get a copyright claim

Email <neonhusky.music@gmail.com> and it will be cleared. That should not
happen; if it does, it is the artist's problem to fix, not the creator's.

## Why CC BY rather than MIT

MIT is a software licence — its text refers to "the Software" throughout, and
its requirement to include the licence text "in all copies" is unworkable for
a video. CC BY is built for creative works, is understood by every platform,
and says what is actually meant here.

## Provenance

The music is AI-generated, produced by Kristian Arvidsson using Mureka. The
per-track PDFs in `licence/` are Mureka **ownership certificates**, not
commercial licences: each certifies the track as an AI music work and that
ownership and IP rights *"(if any)"* belong to the user. That hedge is
Mureka's. CC BY is offered on that basis — as a grant of permission and a
promise not to pursue claims — rather than as an assertion about the
copyright status of AI-generated work, which is unsettled in several
jurisdictions.
